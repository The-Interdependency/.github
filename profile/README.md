# The Interdependency

The Interdependency is an independent research and software organization building
testable systems across agent architecture, formal geometry, measurement,
metadata, language representation, and cross-domain research.

The work is organized around explicit authority boundaries: repositories own their
own claims, implementations, evidence, and limitations. Composition does not
silently transfer proof, empirical validity, security standing, or semantic authority.

## How the repositories relate

No repository is authoritative merely because another repository consumes it.

- **METAPAT** owns its semantic framework.
- **UCNS** owns its geometric constructions and their proof/status boundaries.
- **EDCM** owns measurement and evaluation contracts.
- **skill-lib** owns reusable agent/build/evidence procedures.
- **Stack** composes exact pinned views for cross-repository research without taking over their authority.
- Independent projects graduate from research only when their own repository, verification, release, and reconsumption boundaries are established.

The repository README is the first authority for the current scope and status of
each project.

## Working method

Research and software are developed with:

- explicit assumptions and nonclaims;
- reproducible tests, receipts, and provenance where available;
- preservation of negative results;
- separation of implemented behavior from demonstrated usefulness;
- separation of mathematical construction from empirical validation;
- explicit `hmmm` boundaries where evidence or authority remains unresolved.

Multiple AI systems may participate as computational, drafting, adversarial, and
review tools. They do not become source authority by participation.

## Using the work

Before depending on a repository:

1. read its current `README.md` and governing instructions;
2. check its license and release status;
3. distinguish stable implementation from research candidates;
4. inspect the tests or evidence that support the claim you care about; and
5. preserve any stated nonclaims or unresolved boundaries.

Forks, archives, experiments, and historical repositories remain visible when
they carry useful provenance. Their presence does not make them canonical.
